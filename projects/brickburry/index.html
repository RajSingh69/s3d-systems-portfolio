The project

Brickburry was another of my first commercial website jobs. The aim was practical: make the business look presentable online, organise its information and get the site working properly under its own domain.

What I worked on

I helped with site access, layouts, content, domain setup, SEO basics and ongoing changes. Like Bevells, this was Wix-based work. There wasn't a complicated backend to explain; the work was about getting the details right and delivering something the client could actually use.

My experience

What sticks with me about Brickburry is how hands-on it was. I wasn't disappearing for weeks and coming back with a finished product. I was working with the client, making adjustments and learning how differently people see their own websites. Something that seems minor from a developer's perspective can be the first thing the business owner notices.

What I'd take forward

It made me better at separating technical complexity from client value. A project doesn't have to be complicated to be useful. But I also learned that small jobs can quietly grow, so the time, revisions and responsibilities need to be agreed properly from the start.
