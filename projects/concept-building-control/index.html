The project

Concept Building Control is one of the most involved systems I've taken on. On the surface there's a public-facing website for a building-control business. Behind it is a much bigger piece of work: a private platform to help manage enquiries, projects, documents and the forms that go with a regulated industry.

The challenge wasn't simply making screens look good. I had to understand how the business actually works, who needs to complete which forms, what happens after a quotation is accepted and how information moves between people and systems.

How it works

The public site explains the business and provides a route for enquiries. The private side brings together project records, quotations, client acceptance, Registered Building Inspector forms, documents and activity history. Staff and inspectors have different permissions, and some external form workflows use controlled links rather than asking everyone to sign into the main admin system.

The workflow

Step by step

  1. Project created
  2. Quotation prepared with VAT
  3. Quotation sent to the customer
  4. Customer acceptance
  5. Acceptance PDF generated
  6. RBI checklist completed
  7. RBI PDF generated and stored

Technical deep dive

Under the hood

Platform

The portal uses Firebase Authentication, Firestore, Storage and Cloud Functions, with a custom frontend and PDF/document workflows.

Permissions

Staff and inspectors have different permissions. External form workflows use controlled links, so people who only need to complete a form never get access to the admin system.

AIP integration (in progress)

There has also been work integrating with Barnbrook's AIP software. Reading project and quotation data from AIP has been tested. Writing updates back is still in development and isn't a finished end-to-end integration yet.

My experience

This is the kind of project that really pushed me. Building a dashboard is one thing; understanding building-control processes, document requirements and third-party software is something else entirely. I enjoyed working through the logic and seeing the different parts of the platform start to fit together. At the same time, it showed me that a developer can't just code around a missing API capability or an unclear business rule.

What I'd take forward

I'd establish external-system limitations earlier, agree the exact document flow before implementing it and test every sensitive update against real behaviour rather than relying only on documentation. It reinforced how important permissions, audit trails and careful validation are when software handles real business records.
