The project

London Voices Archive was a very different kind of website. Instead of selling a service or managing business records, it was about presenting stories, places and archive material in a way people would want to explore.

What I built

I worked on a cinematic, editorial-style website with story and collection pages, a London borough map and the supporting information pages. The design needed to let the material do the talking, so the layout, typography and imagery were just as important as the code. It was built with custom frontend code and published through GitHub Pages with domain setup.

My experience

What I liked about this one was the change of pace. Most of my software work is about forms, records and workflows. Here, the challenge was atmosphere and storytelling. The initial build came together extremely quickly, with the site live within days. That was satisfying, but speed only matters if the result is still usable and the content is handled properly.

What I'd take forward

For an archive, the structure has to make sense as more material gets added. I would pay particular attention to content organisation, navigation and keeping images and media manageable over time. The website should still work as an archive, not just look good on launch day.
