The project

SayVah is the app I've been building around a simple idea: if someone needs a hand, there should be an easier way for someone nearby to offer one. It started from the idea of seva and community service, but the platform is designed to be useful across communities, not just for one group.

How it works

Someone posts a request for help and gives the relevant details. Volunteers can find requests, offer to help and, once approved, coordinate through messaging. The app also includes verification, reporting and blocking, moderation, notifications and community features such as local areas, groups and events.

There is more to it than the mobile screens. SayVah has a public website, browser-based features and an administration side.

The workflow

Step by step

  1. Someone posts a request for help
  2. Volunteers find the request
  3. A volunteer offers to help
  4. The requester approves
  5. They coordinate through messaging

Technical deep dive

Under the hood

Mobile app

A Flutter/Dart app that serves both iOS and Android from one codebase, published on the App Store and Google Play.

Backend

A shared Firebase backend using Authentication, Firestore, Storage, Cloud Functions and messaging services, serving the app, the browser features and the admin side.

My experience

SayVah means more to me than a normal software project because I wanted to build something that could genuinely help people. I enjoyed designing the app and working through the technical problems, but the biggest challenge has been everything around the software: trust, safety, moderation and getting real people involved. Releasing an app is a milestone; building an active community around it is a completely different job.

What I'd take forward

I've learned not to judge a community platform by the number of screens it has. What matters is whether people can use it safely, whether requests get real responses and whether the experience is straightforward enough for people to come back.
