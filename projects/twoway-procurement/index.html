The project

TwoWay was one of the biggest pieces of software I worked on in my first year. The business needed a better way to manage procurement, from supplier quotations through to purchase orders and the documents that follow. Instead of information being spread across spreadsheets, emails and separate files, the aim was to bring the process together.

How the system works

A request for quotation starts the process. Supplier prices can be recorded and compared, accepted items move forward into a purchase order, and the system then prepares the commercial paperwork. It handles GBP, USD and CNY, including stored exchange rates, because the values have to remain consistent across the process.

There are dashboards for searching, filtering and tracking records, as well as email notifications. The document-generation side produces purchase orders, invoices, commercial invoices, packing lists and delivery notes using structured templates. A lot of the value is in not having to enter the same information repeatedly.

The workflow

Step by step

  1. A request for quotation starts the process
  2. Supplier prices are recorded and compared
  3. Accepted items move into a purchase order
  4. The order is placed and tracked
  5. Commercial documents are generated

Technical deep dive

Under the hood

Architecture

I built the system around PHP and MySQL, with HTML, CSS and JavaScript for the interface. Business logic runs server-side; records, suppliers and transactions live in a relational database.

Documents and email

Documents are generated from structured spreadsheet templates with exact field placements, so every output matches the layout the business already works with. Notifications are sent with PHPMailer over SMTP.

Hosting

The system runs on cPanel hosting on GoDaddy infrastructure.

The hard part

The difficult part was not choosing a flashy framework. It was making sure the business rules, database records, currency calculations and generated documents agreed with one another. One small error in an accepted item or a conversion flows into every document after it.

My experience

I enjoyed TwoWay because it was proper problem-solving. One small change could affect quotations, totals, documents and the next stage of the workflow, so I had to think about the system as a whole. It was also a step up in responsibility: this wasn't a demo or a university assignment, but software people were going to rely on for actual work.

What I'd take forward

I'd spend more time locking down the workflow before expanding features, and I'd test financial calculations and document templates more systematically. TwoWay taught me that reliability and accuracy matter much more than simply having lots of features.
